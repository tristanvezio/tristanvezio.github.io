Relaciones internacionales y poder

How is power organised in the international system, and what changes when the balance between states begins to shift?

Academic essay · Politics international / Asia and Africa
01 — The question of power

Power as a way to read international politics

This essay examines power as a central concept in international relations, drawing on classical realist approaches associated with Hans Morgenthau and Raymond Aron. It situates the development of international relations as a discipline and considers how power helps explain competition, influence and the conduct of states.

02 — Main lines of analysis
01

Realism and the state

The state, national interest and the pursuit of power as key elements for understanding international relations.

02

Hard and soft power

Joseph Nye’s distinction between coercive resources and influence exercised through attraction.

03

Hegemony and order

Hegemonic power, the transition from bipolarity toward a more multipolar system, and the role of emerging powers.

04

Beyond states

International organisations, regional cooperation and transnational actors as forces shaping the international system.

03 — A changing system

Power is more than military strength

The discussion also considers economic and geographical resources, alliances, international organisations and the limits of a state-centred view. Examples involving the United States, China and Russia, alongside contemporary conflicts, frame the question of how shifts in power can have different consequences for global relations.

The essay brings together competing perspectives on power and international order rather than treating one resource or actor as sufficient to explain global politics.
