Beyond Servitude:
Sayyid Qutb

Sovereignty, freedom and political authority in Milestones (Maʿālim fī al-ṭarīq).

AUTHOR · SAYYID QUTB   |   ORIGINAL PUBLICATION · 1964   |   SUBJECT · ISLAMIC POLITICAL THOUGHT

Introduction

Some books are best approached not simply as historical documents, but as attempts to redefine the political and moral foundations of society. Sayyid Qutb's Milestones is one such work. Written in the context of profound political and intellectual transformations in the Arab world, it offers a distinctive account of authority, human freedom and the relationship between religion and political order.

Reading the text

This review examines the central arguments of Milestones, paying particular attention to the relationship between divine sovereignty and human freedom. Rather than treating the text as a simple political manifesto, it approaches Qutb's work as an intellectual construction whose concepts deserve close examination.

Servitude and freedom

At the centre of this reading is the concept of ʿubūdiyya. Its meaning extends beyond the ordinary idea of servitude. In Qutb's argument, it raises a fundamental question about the source of legitimate authority and the conditions under which human beings can be considered free.

The question is not only who governs, but on what grounds political authority can claim legitimacy.

A critical reading

The significance of Milestones also lies in the questions and tensions it leaves open. What happens to political authority when sovereignty is understood as belonging exclusively to God? How does this conception redefine the relationship between individual freedom, law and society? These questions provide the starting point for a critical engagement with Qutb's text.

Further reflection

This reading considers Milestones not only for its historical influence, but also for the conceptual problems it raises about sovereignty, freedom and political order.
