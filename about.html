About · Background · Research interests

Between histories,
cultures and political ideas.

My path has been shaped by different languages, academic environments and cultural experiences. Having studied in Singapore before pursuing higher education in France and Spain, I have grown up and learned across different cultural settings. Moving between these environments has influenced not only what I study, but also how I approach the questions that interest me.

My academic journey began with History, which I studied through a bilingual French-Spanish programme at the Université Toulouse – Jean Jaurès and the Universidad Autónoma de Madrid. This experience shaped my interest in historical processes, political ideas and the ways societies understand and represent their own transformations.

I subsequently pursued postgraduate studies in International Relations at Blanquerna – Universitat Ramon Llull, exploring geopolitics, international order and political power. My experience at the European Institute of the Mediterranean (IEMed) in Barcelona further developed my interest in regional affairs, particularly through work related to civil society, gender, culture and Euro-Mediterranean relations.

I am currently pursuing a Master's degree in Contemporary Arab and Islamic Studies at the Universidad Autónoma de Madrid. This represents another step in an academic journey increasingly focused on the political, social, religious and intellectual dynamics of the Mediterranean and the contemporary Arab world.

Research interests

At the centre of my interests lies a broader question: how do power, ideas and society shape one another?

I am particularly interested in political thought, ideology and religion; the construction of political authority and legitimacy; conflict and political violence; and the relationship between social movements, political participation and institutions.

I am drawn to questions that cross disciplinary boundaries, especially those connecting historical trajectories with contemporary political realities. The Mediterranean is a particularly important space in this regard: not simply as a geographical region, but as a place of encounters, exchanges, tensions and competing narratives.

Beyond academic boundaries

Studying and living across different cultural and linguistic environments — from Singapore to France and Spain — has encouraged me to approach international affairs with curiosity and an openness to different perspectives.

French and Spanish are the languages in which I have developed most of my academic and personal work. I also work in English and Catalan, while progressively developing my knowledge of Arabic.

Beyond formal academic training, I am interested in reading, writing and exploring ideas independently. I see intellectual curiosity as something that should extend beyond university courses and institutional frameworks.

An evolving space

This website is an independent space for research, writing and reflection.

It brings together academic work, reading notes, research projects and personal reflections. Some texts emerge from university assignments; others develop from independent interests, questions or ongoing readings.

It is not intended as a finished collection, but as a record of an evolving intellectual journey — a place to document what I am working on, what I am questioning and what I am still trying to understand.
