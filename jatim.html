Reading note · Literature · Social representations

Játim

Raja Alem’s novel opens a way to think about identity, social order and the fragile boundaries between belonging and marginality.

Raja Alem, Játim, translated by Milagros Nuin Monreal, Madrid, Huerga y Fierro Editores, 2007.

Beyond the categories

Set in Mecca at the end of the Ottoman period, Játim brings together social realities, religious practices and symbolic worlds. Rather than approaching the novel only as a portrait of a place or period, this reading focuses on the ways in which a community assigns identities, defines acceptable positions and draws the boundaries of belonging.

The individual and the social order

One question guides this reading: what happens when an individual cannot be comfortably situated within the categories through which a society understands itself? The tension between individual experience and collective norms offers a way to examine how identities are produced—not simply discovered—and how social labels can constrain the people they claim to describe.

Marginality can signal exclusion, but it may also expose the limits of the social order that produces it.

A question of marginality

The aim is not to treat marginality as an automatic form of resistance, nor to reduce it to passive exclusion. The more difficult question is how these two dimensions can coexist: how a position imposed from outside may also reveal the instability of the categories that sustain the community.

This is a working reading note accompanying a longer review in preparation. Its argument will be developed through close reading and textual evidence.
