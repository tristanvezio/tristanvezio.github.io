<div class="meta">Research project · 2014–2017 corpus · Tristan Vezio</div>
<article class="body">
<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>This research examines how the Islamic State (ISIS) constructed and articulated radicalization through its English-language magazines <em>Dabiq</em> and <em>Rumiyah</em>, situated within the context of the Syrian Civil War.</p>
<p>Rather than treating propaganda as a simple instrument of recruitment, the study approaches it as a political and ideological discourse through which authority is claimed, identities are defined, and particular representations of violence and legitimacy are produced.</p>
<h2>Research approach</h2>
<p>The project combines critical discourse analysis with a sociology-of-power perspective. It examines the magazines published between July 2014 and September 2017, paying particular attention to the use of religious references, the construction of political and moral boundaries, and the ways in which power and legitimacy are articulated in the texts.</p>
